Race lab

A lap-by-lap race simulator built from public lap data. It is separate from the podium predictions: those come from a model trained on results, this one replays the race. Status: the simulator is built and tested. Strategy and championship tools are not built yet.

What it learned from 80 races

Lap times, tyre stints and pit stops for every race since 2023 come from OpenF1. For each race the lab fits the pace of every driver, how much slower or faster each tyre is, how fast it wears, and how much time a pit stop costs. Wet races (7) are left out, because the tyre model is for dry running. For each target race only earlier races are used.

Does it reproduce real races?

Two checks. The first replays a real race with the real pit stops and retirements, to test the lap-by-lap mechanics. Given the race's own pace the simulator puts finishers in nearly the right order. With pace forecast from earlier races and the qualifying gap, it loses accuracy, so the pace forecast is the weaker part.

As a podium forecaster, the simulator is worse than the podium model and not clearly different from a formula on grid position alone. Brier score difference to the podium model: +0.0059 (95% interval +0.0018 to +0.0101). To the grid formula: +0.0043 (95% interval -0.0022 to +0.0107). Negative means the simulator is better. Intervals come from resampling whole races, on the seasons it was not tuned on.

What this means: the simulator is a fair podium forecaster but not a better one. Its strength is a different one: it produces whole races, so it can test a pit stop plan or play out a season, which the podium model cannot. The next steps build on that.

How it works

  • Each lap a car's time is its pace, plus the tyre offset, plus tyre wear, plus the fuel slope, plus noise.
  • A car cannot pass the one ahead unless it is faster by a margin. Otherwise it is held behind it.
  • A pit stop costs the pit loss and changes the tyre. Under a safety car it costs less.
  • Cars can retire, using each driver's and team's past retirement rate, and a safety car can close up the field, using each circuit's past rate.
  • The whole field follows one typical strategy per simulated race, taken from earlier races at that circuit, with small differences in stop laps.
  • Not modelled: rain, tyre warm-up, damage, penalties during the race, team orders and the effect of fuel on tyre wear.
